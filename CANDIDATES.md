# Candidates & Tournament

Competition: u-coral-ibis-18-deadpan-forecasts-for-cities-that-do-151d4d8c — "Deadpan Forecasts for Cities That Do Not Exist".
Judge brief criteria, in weight order: (1) on-subject forecast for a city that does not exist, absurd yet believable; (2) funnier joke, deadpan twist landing on first read; (3) more vivid detail (concrete image, number or detail); (4) believable broadcast tone; (5) uses its 160 characters well.

All 16 candidates were written fresh in this session (C1 is the previous session's champion, included as one candidate only, as instructed). All are ≤ 160 characters.

## Tournament bracket

Each match is decided on the judge brief criteria in weight order: the first criterion where the two differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.

### Round 1
- **M1: C1 beats C2** — Both on-subject; C1's "rain is scheduled" plus the 1:45 washing deadline is the sharper deadpan joke, with two concrete numbers. C2's frosted mayor statue is gentle by comparison.
- **M2: C3 beats C4** — C3's fog drivers following church bells is a funnier deadpan twist; C4's fish-market breeze is vivid but its joke is mild.
- **M3: C6 beats C5** — C5's ducks moved indoors is good, but C6's rain falling upwards with umbrellas held underneath is the more surprising first-read joke.
- **M4: C8 beats C7** — C7's 214-day drought and alternate-Tuesday cacti are nicely specific, but C8's lightning striking the same lamppost "for the third time this week" is the tighter punchline.
- **M5: C10 beats C9** — C9's sun unseen until Thursday is dry and plausible, but C10's garden sheds moved one street to the left is the more visual, funnier image.
- **M6: C12 beats C11** — C11's clear-below/rain-above inversion is clever, but C12's golf-ball hail plus the golf club asking none be returned is the funnier two-beat joke.
- **M7: C14 beats C13** — C13's egg-frying pavement is a familiar cliché; C14's drizzle ruining "exactly one picnic per household" is the more precise deadpan joke.
- **M8: C16 beats C15** — C15's 4:12pm sunset and curtains-by-four is thin; C16's desert snow leaving the sand briefly white and the camels confused is more vivid and funnier.

### Quarter-finals
- **Q1: C1 beats C3** — Both excellent deadpan; C1 wins narrowly on humour (scheduled rain + washing deadline) and on vivid detail (8 millimetres, 1:45).
- **Q2: C6 beats C8** — Both strong single-inversion jokes; C6's umbrella-underneath instruction is the more original twist.
- **Q3: C12 beats C10** — C10's sheds are vivid, but C12's hail has a second beat (the golf club's request) that makes it funnier and more vivid.
- **Q4: C14 beats C16** — C16's confused camels are vivid, but C14's "exactly one picnic per household" is the sharper deadpan joke.

### Semi-finals
- **S1: C1 beats C6** — C1 has two beats (scheduled rain + washing deadline + polite "thank you" sign-off) and reads most like a real broadcast; C6 is one strong inversion.
- **S2: C12 beats C14** — C14 is precise and quiet; C12's golf-ball hail plus the club's request is the bigger, funnier joke.

### Final
- **F: C12 beats C1** — Both on-subject with strong broadcast tone. Decided on criterion 2 (funnier) and 3 (more vivid): C12's image of hailstones the size of golf balls is more vivid, and its punchline — the golf club asking that none be returned to the course — is the more surprising deadpan twist, landing harder on first read than C1's washing deadline. C1 arguably edges criterion 4 (the "thank you" sign-off), but criteria 2 and 3 come first and differ by more.
